History

Foundation and early years

Inter Milan was founded on 9 March 1908 as Football Club Internazionale, emerging from a split within the Milan Foot-Ball and Cricket Club (now AC Milan). A group of players left the established club because it restricted the signing of foreign players. The founders chose the name "Internazionale" to reflect their commitment to fielding international talent alongside Italian players.

The club won its first championship in 1910 and a second title in 1920. The first championship team was led by Virgilio Fossati, its captain and coach, who later died serving in the Italian army during World War I.

During the Fascist era, in 1928, Inter merged with Unione Sportiva Milanese and was renamed Società Sportiva Ambrosiana for political reasons. The team wore white shirts with a red cross in the 1928–29 season, inspired by Milan's civic symbols. By 1931, supporters' continued use of the "Inter" name led the club to become Associazione Sportiva Ambrosiana-Inter. After World War II, the original name Internazionale was fully restored.

Inter won further championships in 1930 (under Hungarian coach Árpád Weisz, in the first Serie A season), 1938, 1940, 1953 and 1954. Giuseppe Meazza emerged as the club's greatest early star, scoring 284 goals for Inter, a club record, and winning two FIFA World Cups with Italy. His 38 goals in 39 matches in 1929–30 remains the club's record for a single season.

The Grande Inter

In 1960, manager Helenio Herrera arrived from Barcelona and transformed Inter into a dominant European force. Under Herrera, Inter won three Serie A titles in four years, two European Cups and two consecutive Intercontinental Cups. Herrera modified the catenaccio defensive system, originally developed by Austrian coach Karl Rappan, by adding a fifth defender (the sweeper, or libero) behind two centre backs, which gave the team greater tactical flexibility.

Angelo Moratti, who became club owner in 1955, assembled a legendary squad. Spanish midfielder Luis Suárez, the 1960 European Footballer of the Year, joined for a record 25 million pesetas. The core of the team included goalkeeper Giuliano Sarti, full-backs Tarcisio Burgnich and Giacinto Facchetti, sweeper Armando Picchi, playmaker Suárez, Brazilian winger Jair, left winger Mario Corso and inside-right Sandro Mazzola.

In the 1964 European Cup final at the Praterstadion in Vienna, Inter beat Real Madrid 3–1, becoming the first team to win the tournament without losing a single match. Mazzola scored twice and Aurelio Milani added the third. That same year Inter won the Intercontinental Cup against Independiente, losing the first leg 1–0 in Argentina, winning the second leg 2–0 at San Siro, and taking the play-off 1–0 after extra time at the Santiago Bernabéu.

The following year, after eliminating Liverpool 3–0 in the semi-final second leg, Inter beat two-time champions Benfica in the 1965 European Cup final, at home, with a goal from Jair. Inter then defeated Independiente 3–0 in the 1965 Intercontinental Cup, becoming the first European team to win that trophy twice in a row. The club came close to an unprecedented treble that year, having also won Serie A, but lost the Coppa Italia final to Juventus.

The Grande Inter era ended in 1967, when Inter lost the European Cup final 2–1 to Celtic in Lisbon. A week later they lost the Serie A title despite having led the table, and then went out in the Coppa Italia semi-final. It was the club's first season without a trophy since 1961–62.

Colors and badge

Inter's identity centres on black and blue, the club's official colours, which give the supporters their nickname I Nerazzurri ("the Black and Blues"). Another well-known nickname is La Beneamata ("the Well-Cherished One"). The club's badge incorporates Il Biscione ("the Big Grass Snake"), a historic symbol from Milan's civic heraldry that reflects the club's deep connection to the city.

Stadium

Inter shares the San Siro, officially named the Stadio Giuseppe Meazza since 1980 after the death of the legendary forward, with city rivals AC Milan. The arrangement has existed since 1947. San Siro is the largest stadium in Italy, with a current limited capacity of 75,817 and a maximum capacity of 80,018. Inter has the highest home attendances in Italy and the fourth-highest in Europe.

Plans for a new Milan stadium have been discussed to give the club a modern home, but these initiatives remain ongoing.

Supporters and rivalries

The Derby della Madonnina, between Inter and AC Milan, is one of the most widely followed club rivalries in the world. Both teams share the San Siro, which intensifies the fixture.

Inter also contests the Derby d'Italia against Juventus, a rivalry rooted in the long-running competition between two of Italy's most successful clubs. These matches carry significant regional and competitive weight.

Honors

Inter has won 39 domestic trophies in its history, including five consecutive Serie A titles from 2006 to 2010. In 2010 the club also completed an unprecedented Italian treble by winning Serie A, the Coppa Italia and the UEFA Champions League in the same season.
